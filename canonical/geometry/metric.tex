\section{The Classical Metric Tensor $g_{\mu\nu}$}
\label{sec:canonical:metric}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=yellow!8!white,colframe=orange!75!black,title=Derived quantity]
The metric is not an independent UBT field.  It is a real scalar observable
constructed from the existing field $\Theta(q,\tau)$ after retaining and then
averaging its compact $\psi=\operatorname{Im}\tau$ profile.
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Canonical fiber-completed definition}

Let
\begin{equation}
\Theta(x,\psi):=\Theta(q_{\rm GR}(x),x^0+i\psi),
\qquad \psi\in[0,2\pi R_\psi),
\end{equation}
and let
\begin{equation}
\langle A,B\rangle_\eta
:=\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}
\left(A^\ddagger\eta_{\mathbb B}B\right)
\end{equation}
be the fixed real non-degenerate Lorentzian bilinear on the selected
metric-generating representation sector.  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:canonical:metric_from_theta}
\boxed{
g_{\mu\nu}[\Theta](x)
=\frac{1}{\mathcal N_0}\frac{1}{2\pi R_\psi}
\int_0^{2\pi R_\psi}
\langle\partial_\mu\Theta(x,\psi),
       \partial_\nu\Theta(x,\psi)\rangle_\eta\,d\psi,}
\end{equation}
with constant $\mathcal N_0>0$ fixed by the asymptotic Minkowski branch.

\subsection{Immediate properties}

The metric is real and symmetric.  It is non-degenerate and Lorentzian on the
admissible sector for which its Gram determinant is nonzero and its inertia is
$(-,+,+,+)$.  These are conditions on the complete fiber-averaged first jet,
not consequences of nonzero $\Theta$ alone.

The inverse and line element are standard:
\begin{equation}
g^{\mu\rho}g_{\rho\nu}=\delta^\mu_\nu,
\qquad
ds^2=g_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Restricted section projection}

At a chosen phase section $\psi_0$ one may form
\begin{equation}
g^{(\psi_0)}_{\mu\nu}
=\mathcal N_0^{-1}
\langle\partial_\mu\Theta(x,\psi_0),
       \partial_\nu\Theta(x,\psi_0)\rangle_\eta.
\end{equation}
This is a restricted observable.  It is not the metric definition used for the
new off-shell closure theorem because one $\mathbb B\cong\mathbb R^8$ section
has at most four normal closure directions.

\subsection{Normalization correction}

A local denominator
\begin{equation}
\left|\langle\partial_0\Theta,\partial_0\Theta\rangle_\eta\right|
\end{equation}
would force $g_{00}=-1$ on a timelike branch and cannot represent a variable
lapse.  Only the constant $\mathcal N_0$ is canonical in this definition.
