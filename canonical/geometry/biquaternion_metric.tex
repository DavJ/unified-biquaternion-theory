\section{Biquaternionic Quadratic Geometry from $\Theta$}
\label{sec:canonical:biquaternion_metric}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=blue!5!white,colframe=blue!75!black,title=Canonical distinction]
The unprojected product of two biquaternionic derivatives is
$\mathbb B$-valued.  Its scalar part and its ordinary matrix trace are
complex-valued.  These objects must not be conflated.
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Raw biquaternionic quadratic tensor}

Let $\Theta(x,\psi)=\Theta(q_{\rm GR}(x),x^0+i\psi)\in\mathbb B$ and let
$\ddagger$ denote the full Hermitian involution.  The raw quadratic tensor is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:canonical:raw_biq_quadratic}
\boxed{
\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}(x,\psi)
:=\bigl(\partial_\mu\Theta\bigr)
  \bigl(\partial_\nu\Theta\bigr)^\ddagger
\in\mathbb B.}
\end{equation}
It satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathcal Q_{\nu\mu}=\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}^\ddagger.
\end{equation}
The quaternion-vector components, phase components and noncommutative ordering
information live in $\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}$.

\subsection{Scalar Gram channel}

For a fixed real symmetric non-degenerate representation-level bilinear form
$H$ on the metric-generating real sector of $\mathbb B$, define
\begin{equation}
H(A,B)
=\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}
\left(A^\ddagger\eta_{\mathbb B}B\right).
\end{equation}
The scalar Gram channel at fixed $\psi$ is
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{\mu\nu}(x,\psi)
:=H(\partial_\mu\Theta,\partial_\nu\Theta)\in\mathbb R.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, before the final real projection one may retain the complex scalar
$\operatorname{Sc}(\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu})\in\mathbb C$.  Neither
$\operatorname{Sc}$ nor the ordinary matrix trace returns a general
biquaternion.

\subsection{Complex-time-fiber projection}

The fiber-completed classical metric is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:canonical:fiber_metric_from_Q}
\boxed{

=\frac{1}{\mathcal N_0}\frac{1}{2\pi R_\psi}
\int_0^{2\pi R_\psi}
H(\partial_\mu\Theta(x,\psi),\partial_\nu\Theta(x,\psi))\,d\psi,}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal N_0>0$ is a constant fixed by asymptotic normalization.
This uses the existing $\psi=\operatorname{Im}\tau$ dependence of the same
field $\Theta$; it introduces no additional field.

A section metric
\begin{equation}

=\mathcal N_0^{-1}
H(\partial_\mu\Theta(x,\psi_0),\partial_\nu\Theta(x,\psi_0))
\end{equation}
remains a valid restricted observable, but a genuinely $\mathbb B$-valued
single section has an eight-real-dimensional value space and does not provide
generic off-shell $\Theta$-only GR closure.  The full rank theorem is in
\texttt{canonical/gr\_closure/pure\_ubt\_fiber\_closure.tex}.

\subsection{No local lapse normalization}

The older choice
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N(x)=
\left|H(\partial_0\Theta,\partial_0\Theta)\right|
\end{equation}
forces $g_{00}=\pm1$ and is therefore incompatible with a variable lapse.
It is superseded by the constant $\mathcal N_0$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:canonical:fiber_metric_from_Q}.

\subsection{Status boundary}

The current Einstein--Hilbert sector depends on the real metric
$g_{\mu\nu}$, not on a noncommutative determinant or inverse of
$\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}$.  A future action for the non-real components must define
its ordering, inverse, contraction and reality operation explicitly.  Such an
action is not assumed in the present GR closure proof.
