# UBT one-constant rule

<!-- BILINGUAL-UNIT: one-constant-rule.rule -->
## Binding economy rule after adoption

The completed Unified Biquaternion Theory is allowed **at most one independent
continuous physical input coupling**. In the current canonicalization proposal
that coupling is the gravitational response scale

\[
\boxed{\kappa>0.}
\]

This is a theory-design constraint, not a claim that all remaining sectors are
already derived.

The locked normalization `N0` is a unit-setting convention and is not counted
as an independent physical coupling. Discrete choices such as orientation,
signature and representation branch are not continuous constants.

Solution labels and integration constants are also distinguished from action
couplings. A mass, angular momentum or cosmological integration constant may
label a classical solution without becoming a second fundamental constant of
the theory.

<!-- BILINGUAL-UNIT: one-constant-rule.forbidden -->
## No independent sector couplings

No finalized sector may introduce an additional freely fitted continuous
fundamental coupling. In particular, the following quantities must be derived
from the single UBT action, from discrete algebraic/topological data, from a
state or boundary condition, or from the one allowed scale `kappa`:

- gauge couplings and mixing parameters;
- masses and Yukawa-like coefficients;
- coefficients of the `Theta` potential;
- compactification or complex-time length scales if physically observable;
- normalization constants of matter, quantum or statistical reductions.

The local gravity branch does **not** set the cosmological constant to zero.
Instead

\[
\boxed{d\Lambda=0,\qquad\Lambda=\Lambda_0}
\]

follows from the unimodular auxiliary equations, so `Lambda_0` is an integration
constant rather than a second action coupling. A later cosmological theory may
attempt to predict its observed value from global/state/topological data.

<!-- BILINGUAL-UNIT: one-constant-rule.units -->
## Dimensionful units do not multiply the parameter count

A dimensional theory requires a convention for units. Rescaling units may move
powers of `kappa`, `N0`, `c` or `hbar` between formulas without creating new
independent dimensionless information. The parameter count concerns independent
physical inputs in the action after unit conventions are fixed.

<!-- BILINGUAL-UNIT: one-constant-rule.falsification -->
## Precommitted falsification criterion

The one-constant UBT programme fails if a phenomenologically necessary sector
can be made consistent only by adding a second independent continuous action
coupling that cannot be removed by a unit convention and cannot be derived from
the single action or discrete/state data.

An arbitrary fitted `Lambda` term inserted directly into the action would violate
this rule. An integration constant selected by field equations and boundary or
state data does not.
