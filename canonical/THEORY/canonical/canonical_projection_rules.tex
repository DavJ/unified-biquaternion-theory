\begin{abstract}
The historical title of this file referred to ``projection rules.''  The active
canonical geometry no longer projects the metric from a larger tensor.  This
revision distinguishes the exact central metric identity from still-partial
readout maps used in gauge, matter, and observable sectors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Metric: no projection}

\begin{rule}[Central covariant-tetrad identity]
\[
 E_\mu=\mathcal N_0^{-1/2}D_\mu\Theta,
 \qquad
 \boxed{\frac12(E_\mu^\sharp E_\nu+E_\nu^\sharp E_\mu)
       =g_{\mu\nu}\mathbf1.}
\]
On the Lorentz slice this is
$g_{\mu\nu}=e_\mu{}^ae_\nu{}^b\eta_{ab}$.  The metric is the coefficient of
an identity already lying in the real center.  It is not defined by
$\operatorname{Re}$, trace, phase choice, compact-fiber averaging, or an
embedding map.
\end{rule}

\noindent\textbf{Status}: algebraic identity [L0]; local rank ten [L1].

\section{Connection and curvature readout}

The coordinate connection $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$, frame connection
$\omega_\mu{}^a{}_b$, and spin/biquaternionic representation
$\Omega_\mu=\rho_*(\omega_\mu)$ are distinct.  Tetrad compatibility relates
them.  Given tetrad and torsion,
\[
 \omega=\mathring\omega(e)+K(T).
\]
For a two-sided derivative,
\[
 [D_\mu,D_\nu]\Theta
 =F^A_{\mu\nu}\Theta-\Theta F^B_{\mu\nu}.
\]
No componentwise real projection converts one connection or curvature into the
other.

\section{Gauge and matter readouts}

Traces, scalar parts, matrix blocks, chiral projectors, or group representations
may appear in gauge/matter observables when explicitly derived from the action.
They do not redefine the spacetime metric.  Current gauge-field and spinor
identifications remain subject to the proof statuses in `CLAIMS.yaml` and
`STATUS_OF_UBT.md`; an algebraic group embedding is not automatically a full
dynamical Standard Model derivation.

The physical stress-energy tensor, when a matter action is specified, is
defined by Hilbert variation,
\[
 T_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}
 \frac{\delta(\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal L_{\rm matter})}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}.
\]
This is a variational definition, not a projection rule for the metric.

\section{Historical rules}

The former formulas
\[
 g_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{Re}\mathcal G_{\mu\nu},
 \qquad
 g_{\mu\nu}^{(\varphi)}=\operatorname{Re}
   (e^{-i\varphi}\mathcal G_{\mu\nu}),
\]
and compact-$\psi$ fiber averages are retained only in explicitly historical
or exploratory documents.  They must not be cited as the canonical local UBT
metric.
