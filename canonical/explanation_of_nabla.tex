\section{The covariant derivative in the active UBT geometry}

The compact master-equation notation
\[
 D^\dagger D\Theta=\kappa\mathcal T
\]
is meaningful only after the representation and multiplication sides of $D$
are specified.  A covariant derivative compares field values expressed in
local bases that may rotate or boost from point to point.  An ordinary partial
derivative sees both the physical change of the field and the change of the
basis; the connection term compensates the latter.

\subsection{Three distinct connections}

The symbols must not be conflated:
\begin{itemize}
 \item $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$ transports coordinate/spacetime indices;
 \item $\omega_\mu{}^a{}_b$ transports local Lorentz-frame indices;
 \item $\Omega_\mu=\rho_*(\omega_\mu)$ is the spin/biquaternionic
       representation acting on the chosen field space.
\end{itemize}
They are linked by the tetrad-compatibility equation
\[
 \partial_\mu e_\nu{}^a
 -\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}e_\rho{}^a
 +\omega_\mu{}^a{}_b e_\nu{}^b=0,
\]
not by taking a componentwise real part.

For specified tetrad and torsion,
\[
 \omega=\mathring\omega(e)+K(T),
 \qquad K_{abc}=\tfrac12(T_{cab}-T_{abc}-T_{bca}).
\]
Thus the torsion-free classical branch has the Levi--Civita spin connection.
The canonical action must still determine whether torsion vanishes or is
sourced by spin/internal structure.

\subsection{Why the generic curved derivative is two-sided}

Biquaternions act naturally from both sides.  The active curved candidate is
\[
 \boxed{D_\mu\Theta=\partial_\mu\Theta+A_\mu\Theta-\Theta B_\mu.}
\]
Its curvature identity is
\[
 \boxed{[D_\mu,D_\nu]\Theta
 =F^A_{\mu\nu}\Theta-\Theta F^B_{\mu\nu}.}
\]
A one-sided derivative $D^L_\mu\Theta=\partial_\mu\Theta+A_\mu\Theta$ is
allowed in flat or special sectors, but under invertibility and torsion-free
antisymmetric tetrad compatibility it forces its curvature to vanish.  It
therefore cannot be assumed as the generic curved-GR representation.

For the pure Lorentz branch, preservation of the Lorentz slice and metric
already fixes $A_\mu=\Omega_\mu$ and
$B_\mu=-\Omega_\mu^\ddagger$, modulo a common central term that cancels.
This removes any independent $A,B$ field doubling, but it is not the generic
curved solution: with zero torsion it implies
$\nabla_\mu V^\nu=\delta_\mu{}^\nu$ and excludes Schwarzschild with
nonzero mass.  The action-level problem is therefore to derive a nontrivial
relative left/right action beyond the pure spin lift, including any internal
gauge contribution, and to prove that it is composite or auxiliary rather than
propagating.  It must not be inserted by assertion.

\subsection{Special relativity and the implicit curved system}

In Cartesian inertial Minkowski gauge,
\[
 \Gamma=\omega=\Omega=0,
 \qquad D_\mu=\partial_\mu,
\]
and
\[
 \Theta_{\rm SR}=\Theta_0+\sqrt{\mathcal N_0}
 (ix^0\mathbf1+x^k\mathbf e_k)
\]
generates the Minkowski tetrad explicitly.

In curved geometry the reconstructed connections depend on the tetrad that is
itself generated by $D\Theta$.  Hence
\[
 E_\mu=\mathcal N_0^{-1/2}
 [\partial_\mu\Theta+A_\mu[E,T]\Theta-\Theta B_\mu[E,T]]
\]
is an implicit nonlinear first-order PDE/fixed-point system.  A Jacobi-theta
restriction may make the complete system transcendental as well; that does not
remove the separate existence, uniqueness, regularity, and integrability
questions.
