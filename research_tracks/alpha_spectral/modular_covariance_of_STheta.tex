\begin{abstract}
We carry out a complete modular covariance audit of the Unified Biquaternion
Theory action $S[\Theta]$ on the complex-time torus $T^2_\tau$.  Six
requirements are checked in full mathematical detail: (i) identification of
$\tau=t+i\psi$ as a genuine torus modulus, (ii) the $\SL(2,\ZZ)$ Möbius
action on $\tau$ and its physical interpretation, (iii) the induced lattice
relabelling of $\Theta$-modes and the conditions for field-weight consistency,
(iv) transformation of the operator $\nabla^\dagger\nabla$ under modular
reparametrisations and the precise weight obstruction, (v) the Jacobian of the
measure $\dd^4x\,\dd\psi$ and whether compensating factors can cancel,
(vi) the necessary and sufficient condition for equal-action Hecke saddles and
why that condition is currently unmet.

No $\alpha$ input, no fixed-prime input, and no $\eta(i)$ fitting are used.
The verdict class is chosen from the mandatory set
$\{\text{PROVED},\text{CONDITIONAL},\text{NO-GO}\}$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup and notation}
\label{sec:setup}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{The fundamental field}

The fundamental field of UBT is the biquaternion-valued field
\begin{equation}
  \Theta(q,\tau)\in\mathbb{H}\otimes\CC,
\end{equation}
with $q\in\RR^4$ a spatial biquaternion coordinate and $\tau$ the complex-time
parameter.  The four components $\Theta_a\in\CC$ ($a=0,1,2,3$) encode
geometry and all Standard Model fields.

\subsection{Complex time and the torus lattice}
\label{sec:setup:tau}

\begin{definition}[Complex-time parameter]
\label{def:tau}
Let $t\in\RR$ be the real (Minkowski) time and $\psi\in[0,2\pi R_\psi)$ the
compact imaginary-time coordinate with period $2\pi R_\psi$.  Define
\begin{equation}
  \tau \;:=\; \frac{t + i\psi}{\beta},
  \qquad \beta > 0 \text{ (normalization scale)},
  \label{eq:tau-def}
\end{equation}
so that $\Im\tau = \psi/\beta > 0$ for $\psi\in(0,2\pi R_\psi)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
For $\tau$ to parametrise the upper half-plane $\HH$ as a genuine modular
parameter one needs, in addition, a real-time direction that is also periodic,
$t\sim t+2\pi R_t$.  In UBT with a Minkowski real axis this periodicity is
formal; we work in the Euclidean rotation $t\to i t_E$ where both
$t_E\sim t_E+2\pi R_t$ and $\psi\sim\psi+2\pi R_\psi$ are compact and the
torus lattice
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_\tau = \ZZ + \tau\ZZ \subset\CC
  \label{eq:lattice}
\end{equation}
is well-defined.  The analysis below is carried out in this Euclidean
framework; Lorentzian analytic continuation is commented on in
Section~\ref{sec:continuation}.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Canonical action on the extended spacetime]
\label{def:action}
In Euclidean signature the UBT action reads
\begin{equation}
  S[\Theta]
  \;=\;
  \int_{\mathcal M_4\times S^1_\psi}
    \dd^4x_E\,\dd\psi\;
    \Real\,\Tr\!\Bigl[
      \bigl(\nabla^\dagger\Theta\bigr)^\ast\bigl(\nabla^\dagger\Theta\bigr)
      \;-\;
      V(\Theta)
    \Bigr],
  \label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
where $\nabla^\dagger$ is the full covariant biquaternionic gradient (including
Levi-Civita and SM gauge connections), and $V(\Theta)$ encodes mass terms.
The measure $\dd^4x_E\,\dd\psi$ integrates over $\mathcal M_4\times S^1_\psi$.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Source}: \texttt{canonical/THEORY/canonical/canonical\_action.tex},
\texttt{canonical/fields/theta\_field.tex \S3}.
\end{definition}

\section{$\SL(2,\ZZ)$ action on the modulus $\tau$}
\label{sec:SL2Z}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Definition of the modular group action}

\begin{definition}[$\SL(2,\ZZ)$ Möbius action]
\label{def:SL2Z}
For $\gamma=\begin{psmallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{psmallmatrix}\in\SL(2,\ZZ)$
(integers $a,b,c,d$ with $ad-bc=1$), the Möbius transformation is
\begin{equation}
  \gamma\cdot\tau \;:=\; \frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},
  \qquad \tau\in\HH.
  \label{eq:mobius}
\end{equation}
One verifies: $\Im(\gamma\cdot\tau)=\Im\tau\,|c\tau+d|^{-2}>0$, so
$\HH$ is stable.  The group law $(\gamma_1\gamma_2)\cdot\tau=\gamma_1\cdot(\gamma_2\cdot\tau)$
holds.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Physical interpretation}

The Möbius transformation \eqref{eq:mobius} is a reparametrisation of the
torus lattice \eqref{eq:lattice}: it maps the basis $(1,\tau)$ of $\Lambda_\tau$
to the equivalent basis $(c\tau+d,\,a\tau+b)=(c\tau+d)\cdot(1,\gamma\cdot\tau)$,
i.e.\ a rescaling by $(c\tau+d)$ composed with a basis change.  Physically,
for a field theory on the torus $T^2=\CC/\Lambda_\tau$, two values of $\tau$
related by $\SL(2,\ZZ)$ describe the \emph{same} torus with different choices
of cycle labelling.

\subsection{Generators and fundamental domain}

$\SL(2,\ZZ)$ is generated by
\begin{equation}
  T = \begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
  \quad T\cdot\tau = \tau+1,
  \qquad
  S = \begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
  \quad S\cdot\tau = -1/\tau.
  \label{eq:generators}
\end{equation}
The $T$-generator shifts $\psi$ at fixed $t$ (thermal periodicity), while
$S$ inverts the modulus and exchanges the two torus cycles.
The fundamental domain is
$\mathcal F=\{\tau\in\HH:|\Real\tau|\le\tfrac12,\,|\tau|\ge1\}$.

\begin{resultbox}
\textbf{Requirement 1 (\PROVED)}: $\tau=t+i\psi$ (divided by $\beta$) is a
well-defined element of $\HH$ whenever $\psi>0$, and $\SL(2,\ZZ)$ acts on
$\HH$ by Möbius transformations~\eqref{eq:mobius}.  The group law and
$\HH$-stability are standard and hold unconditionally.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Requirement 2 (\PROVED)}: The $\SL(2,\ZZ)$ action on the torus
modulus $\tau$ is the standard Möbius action, generated by $T$ and $S$ as
in~\eqref{eq:generators}.
\end{resultbox}

\section{Induced transformation of $\Theta$ modes}
\label{sec:modes}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Mode expansion on the torus}

Write the field in terms of torus cycle coordinates $(u,v)\in[0,1)^2$
defined by $z = u + \tau v$ (so the lattice points are $m + n\tau$,
$m,n\in\ZZ$):
\begin{equation}
  \Theta(x;\,u,v)
  \;=\;
  \sum_{(m,n)\in\ZZ^2}
  \Theta_{m,n}(x)\;
  e^{2\pi i(mu+nv)},
  \label{eq:mode-expansion}
\end{equation}
where $x\in\mathcal M_4$ and $\Theta_{m,n}(x)\in\mathbb{H}\otimes\CC$.
Translating back to $(t_E,\psi)$ via $u=t_E/(2\pi R_t)$,
$v=\psi/(2\pi R_\psi)$, the modes are Fourier coefficients:
\begin{equation}
  \Theta_{m,n}(x)
  = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^2 R_t R_\psi}
    \int_0^{2\pi R_t}\!\dd t_E
    \int_0^{2\pi R_\psi}\!\dd\psi\;
    \Theta(x;t_E,\psi)\;
    e^{-im t_E/R_t - in\psi/R_\psi}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Lattice relabelling under $\SL(2,\ZZ)$}

Under $\gamma\in\SL(2,\ZZ)$, the torus cycle basis changes as
$(1,\tau)\mapsto(c\tau+d,\,a\tau+b)$, or equivalently
$(u,v)\mapsto(u',v')$ where
\begin{equation}
  \begin{pmatrix}u'\\v'\end{pmatrix}
  = \gamma^{-T}\begin{pmatrix}u\\v\end{pmatrix}
  = \begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u\\v\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad \gamma^{-T}=(\gamma^{-1})^T.
\end{equation}
Since $e^{2\pi i(mu+nv)}=e^{2\pi i\,(m,n)\cdot(u,v)}$ and the exponent must
equal $e^{2\pi i\,(m',n')\cdot(u',v')}$, one obtains
\begin{equation}
  (m',n') = (m,n)\,\gamma^{-T\,-1} = (m,n)\,\gamma.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the mode coefficients transform as
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
    \Theta'_{m,n}(x) = \Theta_{\,(m,n)\gamma^{-1}}(x),
  }
  \label{eq:mode-relabel}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $\gamma$ acts by a lattice relabelling (index permutation) on the
Fourier coefficients.

\begin{lemma}[Mode relabelling is a unitary permutation on $\ell^2(\ZZ^2)$]
\label{lem:unitary}
The map $U_\gamma:\Theta_{m,n}\mapsto\Theta_{(m,n)\gamma^{-1}}$ extends to a
unitary operator on the $\ell^2(\ZZ^2)$-completion of the mode space.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The map $(m,n)\mapsto(m,n)\gamma^{-1}$ is a bijection $\ZZ^2\to\ZZ^2$
(since $\gamma\in\SL(2,\ZZ)$ has integer inverse), and preserves the counting
measure on $\ZZ^2$.  Hence the induced pullback $U_\gamma$ is unitary.
\qedhere
\end{proof}

\subsection{Field-weight condition}

For the field to transform as a modular \emph{form} of weight $(k,\bar k)$
rather than just a scalar, one would need an additional phase factor:
\begin{equation}
  \Theta'_{m,n}(x) \stackrel{?}{=}
  (c\tau+d)^{-k}\,(\overline{c\tau+d})^{-\bar k}\;
  \Theta_{(m,n)\gamma^{-1}}(x).
  \label{eq:weight-cond}
\end{equation}
The UBT canonical formulation does not postulate any modular weight for
$\Theta$.  If $k=\bar k=0$, the relabelling~\eqref{eq:mode-relabel} is the
complete transformation law.

\begin{resultbox}
\textbf{Requirement 3 (\PROVED)}: The induced transformation of $\Theta$ modes
under $\SL(2,\ZZ)$ is the lattice relabelling~\eqref{eq:mode-relabel},
proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:unitary}.  This is an exact, unconditional
statement.  The mode map $U_\gamma$ is unitary.  No hidden weight factor
exists unless one is separately postulated for $\Theta$ — which the current
canonical axioms do not do.
\end{resultbox}

\section{Covariance test for $\nabla^\dagger\nabla$}
\label{sec:operator}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Structure of the operator}

In Euclidean signature with coordinates $(x^\mu,\psi)$ ($\mu=0,1,2,3$),
the kinetic operator splits as
\begin{equation}
  \nabla^\dagger\nabla
  \;=\;
  g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\mu\partial_\nu
  \;+\;
  \frac{1}{R_\psi^2}\,\partial_\psi^2
  \;+\;
  \text{(connection terms)},
  \label{eq:operator-split}
\end{equation}
where the first term is the four-dimensional Laplace–Beltrami operator and
the second is the $\psi$-circle Laplacian.  On a flat background
$g^{\mu\nu}=\delta^{\mu\nu}$, the eigenvalues on the torus are
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_{m,n,\mathbf k}
  = \frac{m^2}{R_t^2} + \frac{n^2}{R_\psi^2} + |\mathbf k|^2,
  \qquad m,n\in\ZZ,\;\mathbf k\in\ZZ^3.
  \label{eq:eigenvalues}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Transformation requirement}

For $S[\Theta]$ to be $\SL(2,\ZZ)$-invariant one needs the full action
integrand to transform with net weight zero.  Since the measure acquires
weight $-2$ (Section~\ref{sec:measure}), the Lagrangian density
$\mathcal L(\Theta,\nabla^\dagger\nabla\Theta)$ must transform with
weight $+2$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L \;\xrightarrow{\gamma}\; |c\tau+d|^{+2}\,\mathcal L.
  \label{eq:Lag-weight-required}
\end{equation}
The kinetic density $\Real\,\Tr[(\nabla^\dagger\Theta)^\ast\nabla^\dagger\Theta]$
acquires weight from $\nabla^\dagger\nabla$ itself.

\begin{proposition}[Operator weight under $T$-generator]
\label{prop:T-covariance}
Under the shift $T:\tau\mapsto\tau+1$ (i.e.\ $t_E\mapsto t_E+\beta$), the
torus spectrum~\eqref{eq:eigenvalues} is invariant because $m,n\in\ZZ$ and
the shift $t_E\to t_E+\beta$ corresponds to $u\to u+1$, which simply cycles
the integer labels. Hence
\begin{equation}
  (\nabla^\dagger\nabla)_{\tau+1} = (\nabla^\dagger\nabla)_\tau
  \quad\text{(on flat background).}
\end{equation}
$T$-invariance of the operator holds on flat background. \PROVED.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Operator transformation under $S$-generator — obstruction]
\label{prop:S-obstruction}
Under $S:\tau\mapsto-1/\tau$, the cycle exchange
$(R_t,R_\psi)\mapsto(R_\psi,R_t)$ maps
\begin{equation}
  \frac{m^2}{R_t^2}+\frac{n^2}{R_\psi^2}
  \;\xmapsto{S}\;
  \frac{m^2}{R_\psi^2}+\frac{n^2}{R_t^2}.
  \label{eq:S-spectrum}
\end{equation}
After mode relabelling $(m,n)\mapsto(n,-m)$ (the $S$ action on the lattice),
the spectrum becomes
\begin{equation}
  \frac{n^2}{R_t^2}+\frac{m^2}{R_\psi^2},
\end{equation}
which equals the original spectrum on the \emph{square} torus $R_t=R_\psi$.
For $R_t\ne R_\psi$ the $S$-transformed operator differs from a rescaled
original by a factor that is \emph{not} a pure power of $|c\tau+d|$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{gapbox}
\textbf{Obstruction O1 (operator weight).}
On a generic background with $R_t\ne R_\psi$, the kinetic operator
$\nabla^\dagger\nabla$ does \emph{not} acquire a definite modular weight
under $S$.  There is no function $w(\tau)$ such that
\begin{equation}
  (\nabla^\dagger\nabla)_{-1/\tau}
  = |c\tau+d|^w\;U_S^{-1}\,(\nabla^\dagger\nabla)_\tau\,U_S.
\end{equation}
This obstruction is absent only on the square torus $R_t=R_\psi$ or
equivalently for self-dual $\tau=i$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What would resolve O1}: Impose the constraint $R_t=R_\psi$
(self-dual torus) as a dynamical condition derived from the UBT field equations,
or promote the field weight to $(k,\bar k)=(-1,-1)$ and show the Lagrangian
density then has weight $+2$.  Neither has been derived from $S[\Theta]$
in the current repository.
\end{gapbox}

\subsection{Curved-background complications}

On a curved background, $\nabla^\dagger\nabla$ includes the Riemann curvature
scalar $R$ and curvature couplings (Weitzenböck decomposition):
\begin{equation}
  \nabla^\dagger\nabla
  = \Box_g + \frac{1}{4}R_g + \text{(gauge curvature terms)}.
\end{equation}
The Ricci scalar $R_g$ depends on the metric $g_{\mu\nu}=\Real(\mathcal G_{\mu\nu}[\Theta])$.
If the metric itself has modular weight (which would follow from $\Theta$ having
a field weight), the curvature terms would also acquire weight.  This chain of
implications requires a consistent weight assignment for the entire $\Theta$
field hierarchy, which is absent from the current canonical axioms.

\begin{resultbox}
\textbf{Requirement 4 (\NOGO)}: On a flat background, $T$-covariance of
$\nabla^\dagger\nabla$ holds (Proposition~\ref{prop:T-covariance}).
$S$-covariance fails generically (Proposition~\ref{prop:S-obstruction}),
unless the self-dual condition $R_t=R_\psi$ is imposed — which is not derived
from the current $S[\Theta]$.  No definite modular weight for
$\nabla^\dagger\nabla$ has been proved.
\end{resultbox}

\section{Transformation of the measure $\dd^4x\,\dd\psi$}
\label{sec:measure}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Jacobian computation}

We work in the Euclidean setting with $(t_E,\mathbf x,\psi)$.
Modular transformation $\gamma\cdot\tau=(a\tau+b)/(c\tau+d)$ at fixed
$\mathbf x$ gives a map on the $(t_E,\psi)$ torus.  Writing
$\tau\to\tau'$ and $t_E+i\psi = \beta\tau$, the rescaling is:
\begin{align}
  \beta\tau'
  &= \beta\,\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}
  = \frac{1}{c\tau+d}\,\bigl(a(\beta\tau)+b\beta\bigr).
\end{align}
The $(t_E,\psi)$-plane undergoes a real linear transformation
(from the real and imaginary parts of $\beta\tau\to\beta\tau'$) with
complex multiplier $1/(c\tau+d)$.  As a real linear map on $\RR^2$ this
has Jacobian determinant
\begin{equation}
  \det_\RR\!\left(\frac{\partial(t_E',\psi')}{\partial(t_E,\psi)}\right)
  = \left|\frac{\dd\tau'}{\dd\tau}\right|^2
  = |c\tau+d|^{-4}.
  \label{eq:jacobian-2d}
\end{equation}
(Each complex derivative contributes $|c\tau+d|^{-2}$; the real determinant
is the square.)

\begin{proposition}[Measure Jacobian]
\label{prop:jacobian}
If $\psi$ is tied to $\Im\tau$ (i.e.\ only $\dd\psi$ transforms, not
$\dd^4 x_E$), then one integrates over $\psi$ at fixed $\mathbf x$ and
fixed Euclidean time $t_E$.  In that case the relevant Jacobian is the
one-dimensional factor
\begin{equation}
  \dd\psi \;\mapsto\; |c\tau+d|^{-2}\,\dd\psi.
  \label{eq:dpsi-transform}
\end{equation}
If instead one performs a joint real-time + imaginary-time modular
transformation (genuine $T^2$-reparametrisation), the full two-dimensional
Jacobian~\eqref{eq:jacobian-2d} applies and
\begin{equation}
  \dd t_E\,\dd\psi \;\mapsto\; |c\tau+d|^{-4}\,\dd t_E\,\dd\psi.
  \label{eq:full-2d-jacobian}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{What cancellation would be needed}

For full $\SL(2,\ZZ)$ invariance of the action integral~\eqref{eq:action}:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Case A} (only $\psi$ transforms): Measure acquires factor
$|c\tau+d|^{-2}$.  The Lagrangian density $\mathcal L$ must transform with
weight $+2$ to cancel.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Case B} (full $(t_E,\psi)$ reparametrisation): Measure
acquires factor $|c\tau+d|^{-4}$.  The Lagrangian density must transform
with weight $+4$.

\medskip
For a scalar field kinetic term on a 2-torus, the heat-kernel argument
(cf.\ Polchinski, \emph{String Theory} Vol.~I, \S7.3) shows that with
proper regularisation the path-integral measure $\mathcal D\Theta$ transforms
with additional modular weight from the functional determinant $(\det'\nabla^\dagger\nabla)^{-1/2}$.
In string theory this weight is exactly cancelled by the ghost determinant
combined with $(\Im\tau)^{-1}$ factors from zero-mode integrations.
In UBT, no analogous ghost or compensator sector is defined.

\begin{gapbox}
\textbf{Obstruction O2 (measure cancellation).}
The measure $\dd^4x\,\dd\psi$ acquires a non-trivial Jacobian under
$\SL(2,\ZZ)$, of the form $|c\tau+d|^{-2}$ (Case A) or $|c\tau+d|^{-4}$
(Case B).  For the action to be modular-invariant, the Lagrangian density
must carry compensating weight.  No such compensation mechanism is derived
from the current UBT action or its functional-measure definition.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{What would resolve O2}: Either (a) assign a definite modular weight
to $\Theta$ and verify that $\Real\,\Tr[(\nabla^\dagger\Theta)^\ast\nabla^\dagger\Theta]$
carries weight $+2$ or $+4$ respectively, or (b) identify a modular anomaly
cancellation between the bosonic and fermionic sectors of $\Theta$ (analogous
to the superstring).  Both are currently absent.
\end{gapbox}

\begin{resultbox}
\textbf{Requirement 5 (\NOGO)}: The Jacobian of the measure $\dd^4x\,\dd\psi$
under $\SL(2,\ZZ)$ is computed explicitly in
Proposition~\ref{prop:jacobian}: it is $|c\tau+d|^{-2}$ (if only $\psi$
transforms) or $|c\tau+d|^{-4}$ (if the full $(t_E,\psi)$ torus is reparametrised).
The compensating weight from the Lagrangian density has not been derived.
Obstruction O2 blocks a proof of measure-covariance cancellation.
\end{resultbox}

\section{Equal-action Hecke saddles}
\label{sec:hecke}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Hecke saddle setup}

Fix a classical saddle $\bar\Theta$ of $S[\Theta]$.  For each
$\gamma\in\SL(2,\ZZ)$, define the modular image saddle
\begin{equation}
  \bar\Theta_\gamma(x;\tau) := U_\gamma\,\bar\Theta(x;\gamma^{-1}\cdot\tau),
\end{equation}
where $U_\gamma$ is the mode relabelling of Lemma~\ref{lem:unitary}.
By definition $\bar\Theta_\gamma$ satisfies the field equations at modulus
$\gamma^{-1}\cdot\tau$.

\subsection{Necessary and sufficient condition for equal action}

\begin{proposition}[Equal-action iff modular invariance of $S$]
\label{prop:equal-action-condition}
$S[\bar\Theta_\gamma]=S[\bar\Theta]$ for all $\gamma\in\SL(2,\ZZ)$ and all
saddles $\bar\Theta$ if and only if $S$ is $\SL(2,\ZZ)$-invariant, i.e.
\begin{equation}
  S[U_\gamma\,\Theta(\cdot;\gamma^{-1}\cdot\tau)]
  = S[\Theta(\cdot;\tau)],\quad\forall\gamma,\,\forall\Theta.
  \label{eq:modular-invariance}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Sufficiency}: If \eqref{eq:modular-invariance} holds, evaluating at
$\Theta=\bar\Theta$ gives $S[\bar\Theta_\gamma]=S[\bar\Theta]$ immediately.

\emph{Necessity}: Suppose equal action holds for all saddles.  By varying
the classical data continuously, the equal-action condition extends (by
density of saddle configurations in the functional space, under appropriate
regularity) to all field configurations, which is~\eqref{eq:modular-invariance}.
\qedhere
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hecke operators and the $p$-winding sector}

At prime level $p$, the Hecke operator $T_p$ acts on modular forms by
summing over the $p+1$ coset representatives of $\Gamma_0(p)\backslash\SL(2,\ZZ)$.
For the saddle count to be $p+1$, one requires the $p+1$ modular images to
be inequivalent \emph{and} equal-action.

The coset cardinality $|\Gamma_0(p)\backslash\SL(2,\ZZ)|=p+1$ is an
exact arithmetic fact (index formula for congruence subgroups).  Physical
inequivalence requires that no residual symmetry maps one saddle to another.
Equal action requires Proposition~\ref{prop:equal-action-condition}, which
requires \eqref{eq:modular-invariance} — which requires Obstructions O1 and
O2 to be resolved.

\begin{gapbox}
\textbf{Obstruction O3 (equal-action Hecke saddles).}
Equal-action Hecke saddles exist if and only if $S[\Theta]$ is
$\SL(2,\ZZ)$-invariant (Proposition~\ref{prop:equal-action-condition}).
That invariance is blocked by O1 (operator weight) and O2 (measure
cancellation).  O3 resolves automatically once O1 and O2 are resolved.
\end{gapbox}

\begin{resultbox}
\textbf{Requirement 6 (\NOGO)}: Equal-action Hecke saddle degeneracy is
not derivable from the current $S[\Theta]$.  The necessary and sufficient
condition (Proposition~\ref{prop:equal-action-condition}) requires
$\SL(2,\ZZ)$ invariance of $S[\Theta]$, which is blocked by O1 and O2.
The saddle count $p+1=|\Gamma_0(p)\backslash\SL(2,\ZZ)|$ is
arithmetically exact and unconditional, but whether the $p+1$ saddles
carry equal weight in the path integral is undetermined.
\end{resultbox}

\section{Lorentzian analytic continuation}
\label{sec:continuation}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The analysis above is in Euclidean signature.  In Minkowski signature, the
real-time direction $t$ is non-compact and $\tau=t+i\psi$ is not in $\HH$
for $\psi=0$.  Three comments:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Thermal interpretation}: If $\psi$ plays the role of
  inverse temperature $\beta$, a \emph{purely imaginary} modular parameter
  $\tau=i\beta/\beta_0$ lies on the imaginary axis, and $T$-periodicity in
  $t_E$ gives the thermal KMS condition.  The $\SL(2,\ZZ)$ analysis applies
  directly in that Euclidean thermal setting.

\item \textbf{Lorentzian non-compactness}: For a genuinely Minkowskian spacetime
  with non-compact $t$, the $S^1_{t_E}$ factor is absent and $\tau$ is not
  a modular parameter in the standard sense.  Modular covariance then
  requires an additional physical input (e.g.\ conformal symmetry in 2d,
  or a compactification of the time direction).

\item \textbf{UBT choice}: In the UBT canonical framework, the real-time
  direction is treated as Lorentzian, with $\psi$ as the compact imaginary
  auxiliary.  For the modular analysis to apply non-trivially in Minkowski,
  either a Wick rotation must be invoked or a separate justification for
  $t$-periodicity must be supplied.  This is an additional assumption beyond
  the current canonical axioms.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What would upgrade the verdict}

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The verdict is currently NO-GO for full modular covariance.  Three specific
constructions could change this:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.2cm, style=nextline]
\item[Path A — Self-dual constraint]
  Show that the UBT field equations on the complex-time torus imply the

  to show O2 also resolves (i.e.\ the Lagrangian density carries weight

\item[Path B — Weight assignment for $\Theta$]
  Postulate (or derive) a modular weight $(k,\bar k)$ for $\Theta$ such
  that $\Real\,\Tr[(\nabla^\dagger\Theta)^\ast\nabla^\dagger\Theta]$ transforms
  with weight $+2$ (or $+4$, matching the measure).  Verify this is
  consistent with the canonical axioms and the GR/SM limits.  If consistent,
  verdict $\to$ \COND\ (pending verification of the limits).

\item[Path C — Anomaly cancellation]
  Identify fermionic and bosonic sectors in $\Theta$ such that their
  combined functional-measure modular anomaly cancels (analogous to the
  superstring).  Requires counting of bosonic and fermionic degrees of
  freedom in $\Theta$ and computing their one-loop modular weights.
  If the anomaly vanishes, verdict $\to$ \COND\ or \PROVED.
\end{description}
