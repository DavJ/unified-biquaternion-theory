# Phase 3 — Spectral-density / degeneracy route

## Objective

Test whether \(B\) is an entropy-like coefficient from internal state counting rather than a bare coupling.

## Generic structure

If mode degeneracy is \(g(n)\), entropy contribution is

\[
S(n)=\log g(n).
\]

To produce the effective potential term, one needs

\[
S(n)\sim B\,n\log n
\]

(or equivalently \(-B n\log n\) in action convention).

## Counting channels tested

1. **Raw compact-mode multiplicity from \(\mathbb C\otimes\mathbb H\), spin/chirality/charge**  
   Leading behavior gives constants or \(\log n\), not unique \(n\log n\) coefficient near 46.28.

2. **Stirling occupancy over sublevels \(k\le n\)**  
   Can produce \(n\log n\), but coefficient depends on occupancy/cutoff dynamics; without independent dynamical derivation this is not closed.

3. **Weyl-law style spectral density**  
   Gives power-law density control and logarithmic residues, but no unique non-fitted constant \(\Delta B\approx 21.151\) from currently fixed canonical inputs.

4. **Ghost / gauge-fixing / chiral subtraction bookkeeping**  
   Changes coefficient candidates but leaves free discrete choices (which modes counted at which scale).

## Phase-3 verdict

A spectral-density interpretation of \(B\) is plausible, but **no zero-choice counting argument presently yields**

\[
B_\mathrm{eff}\in[46.284,46.298]
\]

from canonical field content alone.

**Status:** promising interpretation, not a completed derivation.
