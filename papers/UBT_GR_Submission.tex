\begin{abstract}
Unified Biquaternion Theory (UBT) is a research programme whose canonical
starting point is a single biquaternion-valued field
$\Theta(q,\tau)\in\mathbb C\otimes\mathbb H$ over complex time
$\tau=t+i\psi$.  This paper isolates the local classical gravitational
sector of that programme and formulates its geometry directly from the
covariant first derivative of $\Theta$.  Defining
$E_\mu=\mathcal N_0^{-1/2}D_\mu\Theta$, the classical Lorentz sector is the
real four-dimensional slice
$E_\mu=i e_\mu{}^0\mathbf1+e_\mu{}^k\mathbf e_k$.
Quaternion conjugation gives the exact central identity
\[
 \frac12(E_\mu^\sharp E_\nu+E_\nu^\sharp E_\mu)
 =g_{\mu\nu}\mathbf1,
\]
so the metric is not defined by a trace, real-part map, phase projection, or
imaginary-time fiber average.  The tetrad-to-metric map has rank ten at every
nondegenerate tetrad and a six-dimensional kernel equal to local Lorentz frame
freedom.  We then reconstruct the connection.  For every tetrad and specified
torsion, the metric-compatible Lorentz connection is uniquely
$\omega=\mathring\omega(e)+K(T)$; the torsion-free GR branch is the special case
$K=0$.  Hence the remaining full-UBT question is the dynamics of torsion, not a
kinematically arbitrary $\Omega_\mu$.  We give an explicit affine field
$\Theta_{\rm SR}$ generating the Minkowski tetrad and solving the standard flat
source-free second-order master operator.  Finally, we prove that a naive
one-sided connection with invertible $\Theta$ has a flatness obstruction under
the stated torsion-free compatibility assumptions, while a two-sided
biquaternionic derivative obeys
$[D_\mu,D_\nu]\Theta=F^A_{\mu\nu}\Theta-\Theta F^B_{\mu\nu}$ and permits
nonzero intertwined left/right curvatures.  Lorentz-slice and metric
compatibility reduce its pure no-new-field representative to
$A_\mu=\Omega_\mu$, $B_\mu=-\Omega_\mu^\ddagger$, modulo a cancelling central
term.  We then prove that this pure pairing with $K=0$ makes every
nondegenerate generated tetrad admit a concurrent vector
$\mathring\nabla_\mu V^\nu=\delta_\mu{}^\nu$; it therefore excludes the
non-flat Schwarzschild vacuum exterior with nonzero mass and generic
torsion-free Einstein geometries.  This no-go is specifically torsion-free.
Conversely, on every sufficiently small non-null Gaussian patch we construct
an explicit metric-compatible composite contortion and a Lorentz-real
single-$\Theta$ field satisfying
$D_\mu\Theta=\sqrt{\mathcal N_0}E_\mu$ for an arbitrary smooth tetrad.  Thus
local curved representability is closed without independent $A_\mu,B_\mu$
fields.  Canonical action selection, physical torsion constraints, global
continuation, and Einstein dynamics remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: UBT and the role of \texorpdfstring{$\Theta$}{Theta}}
\label{sec:ubt-theta-introduction}
Unified Biquaternion Theory is a mathematical-physics research programme that
asks whether spacetime geometry and internal field structure can be encoded in
one algebra-valued dynamical object rather than introduced as unrelated
fundamental sectors.  Its canonical starting point is the map
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\Theta:\mathcal M\times\mathbb C_\tau\longrightarrow
 \B=\mathbb C\otimes\mathbb H,\qquad \tau=t+i\psi,}
 \label{eq:theta-overview}
\end{equation}
where $q\in\mathcal M$ denotes a spacetime point, $t$ is physical time, and
$\psi$ is the additional imaginary component of the canonical complex-time
argument.  At each $(q,\tau)$, the value of the field is a biquaternion,
\begin{equation}
 \Theta(q,\tau)=\theta^0(q,\tau)\mathbf1
                +\theta^k(q,\tau)\mathbf e_k,
 \qquad \theta^a\in\mathbb C,
 \label{eq:theta-components-overview}
\end{equation}
so the minimal field value has four complex, or eight real, components.  The
commuting complex unit $i$ and the quaternion units $\mathbf e_k$ play
different algebraic roles.  Their combination supplies complex phase freedom
together with noncommutative quaternionic multiplication and supports the
Lorentz-spin and left/right actions used below.  The symbol $\Theta$ names the
UBT master field; it
does not by itself assert that the field is a Jacobi theta function.  Such
functional ans\"atze may be studied in reduced or spectral models, but no
Jacobi-theta assumption is used in the geometric results of this paper.

Calling $\Theta$ ``fundamental'' means that the canonical programme does not
postulate an independent spacetime metric as a second starting object.  It also
requires any connection, torsion, matter multiplet, or gauge structure used in
a completed theory to be derived from $\Theta$ or justified as a composite or
auxiliary nonpropagating variable.  This is an architectural postulate, not a
claim that all of those derivations have already been completed.

In the gravitational sector studied here, the value of $\Theta$ is not itself
the metric.  The local geometric carrier is its covariant first jet,
\begin{equation}
 E_\mu=\mathcal N_0^{-1/2}D_\mu\Theta.
 \label{eq:intro-tetrad}
\end{equation}
When the four $E_\mu$ lie in the real Lorentz slice of $\B$ and are
nondegenerate, they form a tetrad.  Their complete symmetrized biquaternionic
product is central and defines the metric,
\begin{equation}
 \frac12\left(E_\mu^\sharp E_\nu+E_\nu^\sharp E_\mu\right)
 =g_{\mu\nu}\mathbf1.
 \label{eq:intro-metric}
\end{equation}
The conceptual chain investigated in this article is therefore
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\Theta\ \longrightarrow\ D_\mu\Theta\ \longrightarrow\ E_\mu
 \ \longrightarrow\ (g_{\mu\nu},\omega,R).}
 \label{eq:ubt-geometric-chain}
\end{equation}
The first arrow depends on the representation and connection acting on
$\Theta$; the second identifies the tetrad; the last two are the standard
metric, connection, and curvature constructions once the tetrad and torsion
are specified.

The wider UBT programme also contains proposed gauge, quantum, spectral, and
complex-time research tracks.  They are outside the proof target of this
paper.  The present question is deliberately narrower: whether the single-field
ansatz supplies a mathematically consistent local Lorentzian geometry, which
parts of the GR tetrad chain follow kinematically, and which steps still require
a canonical action and dynamical proof.  In particular, this article does not
claim a completed derivation of Einstein dynamics or of the Standard Model.

\section{Biquaternionic algebra and the Lorentz slice}
Let $\B=\mathbb C\otimes\mathbb H$, with commuting complex unit $i$ and
quaternion units
\[
 \mathbf e_j\mathbf e_k=-\delta_{jk}\mathbf1
 +\epsilon_{jk\ell}\mathbf e_\ell.
\]
Quaternion conjugation $\sharp$ reverses the quaternion units without
conjugating the commuting complex coefficients:
\[
 (z^0\mathbf1+z^k\mathbf e_k)^\sharp
 =z^0\mathbf1-z^k\mathbf e_k.
\]

\begin{definition}[Classical Lorentz module]
\[
 W_L=\{i x^0\mathbf1+x^k\mathbf e_k\mid x^a\in\R\}.
\]
For $X=i x^0\mathbf1+x^k\mathbf e_k$ and
$Y=i y^0\mathbf1+y^k\mathbf e_k$, let
\[
 \eta(X,Y)=-x^0y^0+x^ky^k.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Central anticommutator identity]
For all $X,Y\in W_L$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\frac12(X^\sharp Y+Y^\sharp X)=\eta(X,Y)\mathbf1.}
 \label{eq:central}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In scalar--vector notation, quaternion multiplication is
$(a,\mathbf u)(b,\mathbf v)=(ab-\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v,
 a\mathbf v+b\mathbf u+\mathbf u\times\mathbf v)$.
For $X=(ix^0,\mathbf x)$, $X^\sharp=(ix^0,-\mathbf x)$.  The scalar part of
$X^\sharp Y$ is $-x^0y^0+\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf y$.  The vector parts of
$X^\sharp Y$ and $Y^\sharp X$ are opposites, including the cross products, so
they cancel in the half-sum.
\end{proof}

\section{The covariant UBT tetrad}
The fundamental field remains
\[
 \Theta(q,\tau)\in\B,\qquad \tau=t+i\psi.
\]
On physical spacetime it is assumed smooth.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{E_\mu:=\mathcal N_0^{-1/2}D_\mu\Theta,}
 \label{eq:E}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal N_0>0$ is fixed globally and $D_\mu$ is a covariant derivative.
The connection is kept representation-independent at this stage:
\begin{equation}
 D_\mu\Theta=\partial_\mu\Theta+\rho_*(\Omega_\mu)\Theta.
 \label{eq:D}
\end{equation}
The map $\rho_*$ must be specified in each concrete representation; Eq.~\eqref{eq:D}
does not assume left, right, or two-sided action prematurely.

\begin{definition}[Admissible classical tetrad]
A configuration belongs to the classical tetrad sector if
\[
 E_\mu=i e_\mu{}^0\mathbf1+e_\mu{}^k\mathbf e_k\in W_L
\]
for all $\mu$ and the matrix $e_\mu{}^a$ is invertible.
\end{definition}

\section{Projection-Free Metric and Algebraic Bivector}
By Theorem~1,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\frac12(E_\mu^\sharp E_\nu+E_\nu^\sharp E_\mu)
       =g_{\mu\nu}\mathbf1,}
 \label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 g_{\mu\nu}=e_\mu{}^ae_\nu{}^b\eta_{ab},
 \qquad \eta_{ab}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1).
 \label{eq:tetradmetric}
\end{equation}
The complete left-hand side is already a central real element.  Hence
Eq.~\eqref{eq:metric} defines the metric without discarding or projecting
noncentral components.  The fixed constant $\mathcal N_0$ only sets units.

The complementary antisymmetric product is
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma_{\mu\nu}
 :=\frac12(E_\mu^\sharp E_\nu-E_\nu^\sharp E_\mu),
 \qquad \Sigma_{\nu\mu}=-\Sigma_{\mu\nu}.
 \label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
Thus one product contains both the symmetric central geometry and an oriented
bivector sector.  No gauge-field identification of $\Sigma_{\mu\nu}$ is made
without a separate transformation and dynamical analysis.

\section{The local rank theorem}
Define $\Phi(e)=e\eta e^{\mathsf T}$.

\begin{theorem}[Full local metric rank]
At every invertible tetrad, the differential
$d\Phi_e:\R^{4\times4}\to\operatorname{Sym}_4(\R)$ is surjective and has rank
ten.  Its kernel is the six-dimensional local Lorentz algebra.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first variation is
\[
 \delta g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{ab}
 (\delta e_\mu{}^a e_\nu{}^b+e_\mu{}^a\delta e_\nu{}^b).
\]
For an arbitrary symmetric $h_{\mu\nu}$ choose
\[
 \delta e_\mu{}^a=\frac12h_{\mu\rho}e^{\rho a}.
\]
Then $\delta g_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu\nu}$.  Hence the differential is surjective and
has rank ten.  The domain has dimension sixteen, leaving a six-dimensional
kernel.  The transformations $\delta e=e\lambda$ with
$\lambda^{\mathsf T}\eta+\eta\lambda=0$ lie in the kernel and constitute the
six-dimensional Lorentz Lie algebra.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
The local comparison ``a biquaternion has eight real components, whereas a
metric has ten'' is not the relevant rank count.  The metric depends on four
covariant first derivatives, which form a sixteen-component real tetrad before
local Lorentz redundancy is removed.
\end{corollary}

This theorem closes the local algebraic rank question.  It does not prove that
all tetrad variations arise from admissible variations of one on-shell
$\Theta$ and its derived connection.

\section{Connection and Christoffel symbols}
The Christoffel symbols and the frame connection describe different indices:
\begin{itemize}
 \item $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$ transports coordinate indices;
 \item $\omega_\mu{}^a{}_b$, and its spin/biquaternionic lift $\Omega_\mu$,
       transport the local Lorentz frame.
\end{itemize}
They are related by
\begin{equation}
 \partial_\mu e_\nu{}^a-\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}e_\rho{}^a
 +\omega_\mu{}^a{}_b e_\nu{}^b=0.
 \label{eq:tetradpostulate}
\end{equation}
It is generally incorrect to identify $\Gamma$ with a real component of
$\Omega$.

\begin{theorem}[Unique torsion-free classical frame connection]
\label{thm:omega-unique-paper}
For every nondegenerate $C^2$ tetrad, metric compatibility and vanishing
torsion determine a unique Lorentz connection,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \mathring\omega_\mu{}^a{}_b
 =e_b{}^\nu\left(
 \mathring\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}(g)e_\rho{}^a
 -\partial_\mu e_\nu{}^a
 \right),}
 \label{eq:omega-explicit-paper}
\end{equation}
with $\mathring\omega_{\mu ab}=-\mathring\omega_{\mu ba}$.  Its spin lift is
unique in the chosen representation, up to ordinary local Lorentz gauge.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Levi--Civita connection $\mathring\Gamma(g)$ is uniquely fixed by
$\mathring\nabla g=0$ and
$\mathring\Gamma^\rho{}_{[\mu\nu]}=0$.  Solving
Eq.~\eqref{eq:tetradpostulate} gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega-explicit-paper}.  If two Lorentz connections have the same
zero torsion, their difference is antisymmetric in its first two frame indices
and symmetric in the indices forced by equality of torsion; cycling these
symmetries gives zero.
\end{proof}

The torsion-free result extends to arbitrary specified torsion.  Write
\begin{equation}
 T^a=de^a+\omega^a{}_b\wedge e^b
 =\frac12T^a{}_{bc}e^b\wedge e^c.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Tetrad--torsion reconstruction]
For every nondegenerate tetrad and specified torsion, there is exactly one
metric-compatible Lorentz connection,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\omega^a{}_b=\mathring\omega^a{}_b(e)+K^a{}_b(T),}
 \label{eq:omega-torsion-paper}
\end{equation}
where, with the convention above,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 K_{abc}=\frac12\left(T_{cab}-T_{abc}-T_{bca}\right).}
 \label{eq:K-paper}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Metric compatibility makes $K_{abc}$ antisymmetric in $a,b$.  Since the
Levi--Civita part has zero torsion,
$T_{abc}=K_{acb}-K_{abc}$.  Combining this equation with its cyclic
permutations yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:K-paper}, proving existence and uniqueness.
\end{proof}

Thus $\Omega_\mu$ is not an additional arbitrary classical matter field once
the tetrad and torsion are fixed.  The full-UBT problem is to derive $T$ from
the action.  In the GR vacuum branch $T=0$, so $K=0$.  Because every
metric-compatible $\omega_\mu$ lies in $\mathfrak{so}(1,3)$, its parallel
transport preserves $\eta_{ab}$ and the Lorentz slice, including in branches
with nonzero contorsion.

\section{Curvature and special relativity}
For a one-sided representation, the curvature is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_{\mu\nu}
 =\partial_\mu\Omega_\nu-\partial_\nu\Omega_\mu
 +[\Omega_\mu,\Omega_\nu].
 \label{eq:curvature}
\end{equation}
It must be distinguished from the algebraic tetrad bivector
$\Sigma_{\mu\nu}$.

In a flat simply connected region, zero curvature permits a local frame with
$\Omega_\mu=0$.  In inertial Cartesian coordinates one can also set
$\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}=0$.  The flat limit has an explicit single-field
representer.

\begin{theorem}[Affine representer of a constant Lorentz tetrad]
Let $\bar E_\mu\in W_L$ be constant and linearly independent.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \Theta_{\rm aff}(x)=\Theta_0+
 \sqrt{\mathcal N_0}\,\bar E_\mu x^\mu}
 \label{eq:affine-paper}
\end{equation}
in the inertial gauge satisfies
$\mathcal N_0^{-1/2}\partial_\mu\Theta_{\rm aff}=\bar E_\mu$ and induces the
constant metric determined by the central anticommutator.
\end{theorem}

For
\begin{equation}
 \bar E_0=i\mathbf1,\qquad \bar E_k=\mathbf e_k,
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:affine-paper} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \Theta_{\rm SR}=\Theta_0+
 \sqrt{\mathcal N_0}\left(i x^0\mathbf1+x^k\mathbf e_k\right),}
\end{equation}
which generates $g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}$.  Every second spacetime derivative
vanishes, so the standard flat source-free constant-coefficient second-order
master operator annihilates $\Theta_{\rm SR}$.  Nonzero connection
coefficients in curvilinear coordinates do not by themselves imply curvature.

\section{Integrability and representation selection}
The tetrad is not independent in UBT; it must satisfy
$E_\mu=\mathcal N_0^{-1/2}D_\mu\Theta$.  Once the classical connection is
reconstructed from $E$ and torsion, this becomes an implicit nonlinear
first-order system in which the unknown tetrad occurs on both sides.

\subsection{One-sided flatness obstruction}
Suppose
\begin{equation}
 D^L_\mu\Theta=\partial_\mu\Theta+A_\mu\Theta,
 \qquad
 [D^L_\mu,D^L_\nu]\Theta=F^L_{\mu\nu}\Theta.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[One-sided invertible-field no-go]
Assume $E_\nu=\mathcal N_0^{-1/2}D^L_\nu\Theta$, torsion-free antisymmetric
tetrad compatibility, the same induced one-sided connection on
$D_\nu\Theta$, and invertible $\Theta$.  Then $F^L_{\mu\nu}=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Torsion-free compatibility makes the antisymmetrised covariant Hessian vanish,
so $F^L_{\mu\nu}\Theta=0$.  Right multiplication by $\Theta^{-1}$ gives
$F^L_{\mu\nu}=0$.
\end{proof}

Thus the naive one-sided regular representation cannot be the generic curved
GR route for invertible $\Theta$.  A noninvertible stabilizer sector is a
possible exceptional branch, not a representation of arbitrary geometries.

\subsection{Two-sided biquaternionic derivative}
The algebra $\mathbb B$ is naturally a bimodule over itself.  Consider
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 D_\mu\Theta=\partial_\mu\Theta+A_\mu\Theta-\Theta B_\mu.}
 \label{eq:two-sided-paper}
\end{equation}
A direct expansion gives
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 [D_\mu,D_\nu]\Theta
 =F^A_{\mu\nu}\Theta-\Theta F^B_{\mu\nu}.}
 \label{eq:two-sided-curvature-paper}
\end{equation}
Torsion-free antisymmetric compatibility therefore reduces to
\begin{equation}
 F^A_{\mu\nu}\Theta=\Theta F^B_{\mu\nu}.
 \label{eq:intertwiner-paper}
\end{equation}
For invertible $\Theta$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{F^A_{\mu\nu}=\Theta F^B_{\mu\nu}\Theta^{-1}.}
\end{equation}
The left and right curvatures may be nonzero.  The field $\Theta$ acts as an
intertwiner between them.  This does not prove existence for an arbitrary
curved tetrad; it only exhibits an algebra-native escape from the stated
one-sided flatness obstruction.

\subsection{Pure Lorentz-compatible pair and no-new-field reduction}
Let $\ddagger$ denote Hermitian conjugation and
$W_L=\{X\in\B\mid X^\ddagger=-X\}$.  Requiring Eq.~\eqref{eq:two-sided-paper}
to preserve $W_L$ for every $X\in W_L$ is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
 B_\mu=-A_\mu^\ddagger+c_\mu\mathbf1,
 \qquad c_\mu\in\R.
 \label{eq:slice-preserving-pair-paper}
\end{equation}
If the same transport preserves the Lorentz quadratic form, the real dilation
vanishes.  Splitting off the trace then gives, modulo a common central
one-form that cancels identically,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{A_\mu=\Omega_\mu,\qquad B_\mu=-\Omega_\mu^\ddagger,}
 \label{eq:pure-pair-paper}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
 D_\mu X=\partial_\mu X+\Omega_\mu X+X\Omega_\mu^\ddagger.
 \label{eq:vector-spin-lift-paper}
\end{equation}
Thus $A_\mu$ and $B_\mu$ are not two new gravitational fields in the pure
Lorentz branch.  They are the left and right module actions of the one spin
connection reconstructed from tetrad and torsion.

\begin{theorem}[Concurrent-vector obstruction]
Assume Eq.~\eqref{eq:vector-spin-lift-paper}, zero torsion, and a nondegenerate
tetrad satisfying
$D_\mu\Theta=\sqrt{\mathcal N_0}E_\mu$.  Then the Lorentz-real projection of
$\Theta$ defines a spacetime vector $V$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\nabla_\mu V^\nu=\delta_\mu{}^\nu.}
 \label{eq:concurrent-paper}
\end{equation}
Consequently $\mathcal L_Vg=2g$ and
$R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The derivative in Eq.~\eqref{eq:vector-spin-lift-paper} commutes with the
Lorentz involution $\mathcal JX=-X^\ddagger$.  Hence
$X=(\Theta+\mathcal J\Theta)/2\in W_L$ obeys
$D_\mu X=\sqrt{\mathcal N_0}E_\mu$.  Writing
$X=X^a\mathbf u_a$, $E_\mu=e_\mu{}^a\mathbf u_a$ and defining
$V^\nu=\mathcal N_0^{-1/2}e_a{}^\nu X^a$, the inverse tetrad postulate gives
\[
 \nabla_\mu V^\nu
 =\mathcal N_0^{-1/2}e_a{}^\nu D_\mu X^a
 =e_a{}^\nu e_\mu{}^a
 =\delta_\mu{}^\nu.
\]
The homothety and curvature-kernel identities follow by symmetrising and
antisymmetrising covariant derivatives.
\end{proof}

For the non-flat Schwarzschild vacuum exterior
$ds^2=-fdt^2+f^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2$ with
$f=1-2M/r$, rotational averaging reduces any putative homothetic vector to
$V=T(t,r)\partial_t+R(t,r)\partial_r$.  The angular equation gives $R=r$,
while the $rr$ equation gives $f'(r)=0$, contradicting
$f'=2M/r^2$ for $M\ne0$.  Therefore the pure pair is closed as a kinematic
no-new-field reduction and its $K=0$ branch is closed as a torsion-free no-go.
This statement concerns the vacuum exterior geometry; it does not depend on
whether an exactly nonrotating astrophysical black hole is realised.

\subsection{Composite-contortion local curved representer}
The torsion-free qualifier is essential.  Let $\rho$ be a shifted non-null
Gaussian coordinate on a sufficiently small patch,
\begin{equation}
 g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\rho\partial_\nu\rho=\varepsilon,
 \qquad \varepsilon=\pm1,
 \qquad \rho\ne0,
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}
 V^\mu=\varepsilon\rho\nabla^\mu\rho.
\end{equation}
Then $V^2=\varepsilon\rho^2$ and
$V_\mu=\tfrac12\partial_\mu(V^2)$.  Put
\begin{equation}
 W_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}-\mathring\nabla_\mu V_\nu,
 \qquad
 \boxed{K_{\nu\mu\rho}
 =\frac{W_{\mu\nu}V_\rho-V_\nu W_{\mu\rho}}{V^2}.}
 \label{eq:composite-K-paper}
\end{equation}
Because $W_{\mu\nu}V^\nu=0$, the tensor in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:composite-K-paper} obeys
$K_{\nu\mu\rho}=-K_{\rho\mu\nu}$ and is therefore a metric-compatible
contortion.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 K^\nu{}_{\mu\rho}V^\rho=W_\mu{}^\nu,
 \qquad
 \nabla^{(\mathring\Gamma+K)}_\mu V^\nu=\delta_\mu{}^\nu.
 \label{eq:torsionful-concurrent-paper}
\end{equation}
Let $V^a=e^a{}_{\nu}V^\nu$ and define
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\Theta=\sqrt{\mathcal N_0}\,V^a\mathbf u_a.}
 \label{eq:torsionful-theta-paper}
\end{equation}
The tetrad postulate for $\omega(e,K)$ then gives
\begin{equation}
 D_\mu\Theta
 =\sqrt{\mathcal N_0}\,e^a{}_{\nu}
   \nabla^{(\mathring\Gamma+K)}_\mu V^\nu\mathbf u_a
 =\sqrt{\mathcal N_0}E_\mu.
\end{equation}
Thus every smooth Lorentzian tetrad has a local single-$\Theta$ representer
inside the same one-connection pairing
$A=\Omega(e,K)$, $B=-\Omega(e,K)^\ddagger$.  No independent $A_\mu$ or
$B_\mu$ field is introduced.  On a Schwarzschild exterior patch $r>2M$, one
may take
\begin{equation}
 \rho(r)=\rho_0+\int_{r_0}^{r}
 \frac{du}{\sqrt{1-2M/u}},
 \qquad

This explicitly represents the non-flat vacuum exterior locally, but with
generally nonzero composite torsion.

A relative pair may still be parameterised as

\subsection{Exact prescribed-connection integrability criterion}

 Y=\binom{\Theta}{1},\qquad
 \widehat{\mathcal C}_\mu=
 \begin{pmatrix}\mathcal C_\mu&-sE_\mu\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:augmented-paper}
\end{equation}

 \widehat{\mathcal F}_{\mu\nu}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \mathcal F_{\mu\nu}&
 -s(\mathcal D_\mu E_\nu-\mathcal D_\nu E_\mu)\\0&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \label{eq:augmented-curvature-paper}

where $\mathcal F_{\mu\nu}X=F^A_{\mu\nu}X-XF^B_{\mu\nu}$.

simply connected domain, flat augmented curvature is a sufficient condition
for every initial value.  Thus the prescribed-coefficient integrability
problem is closed.  GAP-10I-CURVED now has local kinematic existence closed
by the composite-contortion construction.  What remains is the UBT-specific

\section{Complex time and regularity}
The field may depend on $\tau=t+i\psi$.  The metric definition

classical four-dimensional sector may require

condition.

Smoothness of the physical spacetime field and intrinsic biquaternionic

structures must be compared before a regularity axiom is locked.

This structure resolves the local rank objection without auxiliary maps,
metric projections, or fiber averaging.  The connection is also no longer an
arbitrary addition: for specified tetrad and torsion it is uniquely

the concurrent-vector theorem, but an explicit composite contortion provides
a local single-$\Theta$ right inverse for every smooth tetrad.  The decisive
remaining work is therefore dynamical rather than local kinematic: derive from
the single UBT action whether this composite torsionful branch or an optional
torsion-free relative bimodule branch is selected, establish algebraic
nonpropagation and physical torsion bounds, derive the minimal first-order/
